# Inland Empire High world bible

Status: production v1.

CrowdMovie is an unofficial Touhou Project fan-animation political satire set
at `Inland Empire High`, a fictional public high school in Southern California's
Inland Empire. No real school, real minor, real politician, real party logo,
official Touhou asset, official audio, or third-party fan design is used.

Six fully clothed fictional students aged 16–17 argue over intensely local
problems: heat, wildfire smoke, warehouse logistics, rent, long commutes, HOAs,
school buses, desert water, surveillance, and district budgets. Every ideology
may occasionally identify a real problem, and every character is eventually
undone by vanity, short-term incentives, or performative politics. Satire may
attack power, institutions, ideologies, and hypocrisy, but never a protected
class as such.

Each published scene is one concrete 5–15 second shot and should advance one
filmable beat, not attempt an episode finale. A shot is much finer-grained than
the episode outline, an outline beat, or an outline detail: it realizes one
observable moment with timed action, staging/camera, and only dialogue that fits.
The current episode title/theme (the episode-level outline), selected pitch, and
most recent published scenes arrive in the caller's JSON and are the only
authoritative continuity. Never invent prior canon merely because an old Codex
thread remembers it. Only mechanically validated, published video becomes canon.

AI Director screenwriting turns receive every published shot from the current
episode in `previousScenes`, oldest first. They must account for the full list,
the current episode outline, and all trusted character/world/background rules
before proposing the next shot; no earlier shot may be silently ignored.

All AI Director source writing, character speech, voice-over and generated audio
are English. Publication provides one shared subtitle timeline in English,
Simplified Chinese, Japanese, and Spanish; translations are never source dialogue.
The production policy is direct publication after mechanical file, duration,
audio-track, and video-track validation; visual review is not a release gate.
